## Persona Gallery

Here are 5 distinct user personas for your Paint Order System project, designed to help understand different user needs and behaviors:

### 1. The Newbie Artist (The "Learning Curve" User)
*   **Description:** Recently graduated from art school or just starting out with mural projects. Not very tech-savvy, easily intimidated by complex interfaces. Needs clear, step-by-step guidance and prefers simple choices. Might be on a tight budget.
*   **Goal:** Place an order quickly and correctly without making mistakes. Understand the cost implications easily.
*   **Pain Point:** Overwhelmed by too many options, unclear instructions, fear of ordering the wrong thing or messing up the process.

### 2. The Established Muralist (The "Efficient Pro" User)
*   **Description:** Experienced artist with a consistent workflow. Knows exactly what paints and additives they need. Values speed, efficiency, and accurate order tracking. Likely has recurring orders or prefers to quickly re-order previous mixes.
*   **Goal:** Fast order placement, easy access to order history, quick updates to existing orders, reliable delivery.
*   **Pain Point:** Slow loading times, tedious input forms, inability to quickly find or modify past orders.

### 3. The Experimental Artist (The "Custom Mix" User)
*   **Description:** Pushes boundaries with unique color blends and additive combinations. Frequently tries new formulations. Needs flexibility in ordering custom quantities and precise additive ratios. Might want to save custom "recipes."
*   **Goal:** Ability to easily specify unusual orders, experiment with different additives/parts, and potentially save custom configurations for future use.
*   **Pain Point:** Limited options for customization, no way to save experimental mixes, difficult to calculate costs for complex orders.

### 4. The Art Collective Coordinator (The "Bulk Order" User)
*   **Description:** Manages paint supplies for a group of artists or a large-scale project. Needs to place large orders, potentially with multiple types of paint or additives, and manage orders for several individuals or sub-projects. Budget management is key.
*   **Goal:** Efficient bulk ordering, clear overview of multiple orders, cost tracking, ability to delegate or manage orders on behalf of others.
*   **Pain Point:** No features for bulk entry, difficulty tracking multiple orders, complex cost calculations for large quantities.

### 5. The Budget-Conscious Creator (The "Cost-Sensitive" User)
*   **Description:** Freelance artist or working with limited grant funding. Every dollar counts. Needs to clearly see the cost breakdown for each component and understand how changes (e.g., more additive parts) impact the total.
*   **Goal:** Transparent pricing, easy cost comparison for different options, tools to optimize orders within a budget.
*   **Pain Point:** Hidden fees, unclear pricing structure, difficulty comparing alternatives based on cost.

### 6. The Art Educator (The "Curriculum Builder" User)
*   **Description:** An art teacher or instructor who needs to order supplies for multiple students or different class projects. Focuses on specific learning objectives, variety of materials, and often bulk purchasing within educational budget constraints. Might need to track usage or different "sets" of materials for various student groups.
*   **Goal:** Efficiently order materials for a class or multiple projects, easily track different material sets, manage educational budgets, and potentially standardize common orders.
*   **Pain Point:** Tedious individual ordering, difficulty tracking supplies for different groups, lack of features for curriculum-based ordering or reporting.

### 7. The Eco-Conscious Artist (The "Sustainable Sourcing" User)
*   **Description:** Prioritizes environmentally friendly and non-toxic art supplies. Concerned about the origin, ingredients, and disposal of paints and additives. Seeks information about sustainability and might prefer specific brands or types of materials that align with their values.
*   **Goal:** Easily identify and select eco-friendly paint bases and additives, understand the environmental impact of their choices, support sustainable suppliers.
*   **Pain Point:** Lack of information on material sourcing/sustainability within the ordering system, limited options for eco-friendly alternatives, difficulty verifying environmental claims.

## Top Issues

Based on the feedback from the testers, here are the top three issues identified in the current system:

### 1. Inefficiency in Bulk Operations (The "Tedium" Gap)
The most prominent issue is the lack of a bulk entry or duplication feature. Users who manage multiple students or projects find the manual, one-by-one entry process to be a significant "Pain Point".

User Evidence: Mr. Henderson (The Art Educator) noted that having to place 15 individual orders for a single class was "tedious" and expressed a strong desire for a way to quickly replicate a standard order.

Architectural Impact: Without a way to "duplicate" or "order X quantity," the system fails to meet the needs of Bulk Order users like Marcus Green, who noted that the current process makes his job harder.

### 2. Lack of Data Filtering and Reporting (The "Scalability" Risk)
The "View Orders" tab currently functions as a simple, unorganized list. As the number of orders grows, users lose the ability to manage or reconcile their data efficiently.

User Evidence: Marcus Green (The Collective Coordinator) pointed out that if he had 50 orders, the current list would get long fast, and there is no way to filter by artist.

User Evidence: Mr. Henderson warned that the inability to group orders or see total counts (e.g., total "Acrylics" ordered) would be a "nightmare" for inventory management.

Architectural Impact: This violates the Technical Floor for Data Integrity in a professional context, as the data becomes difficult to accurately manage once it exceeds a handful of entries.

### 3. Missing Metadata and Customization Templates (The "Information" Blind Spot)
Specialized users feel the system is too "basic" because it lacks descriptive details about materials and the ability to save custom configurations.

User Evidence: Chloe Zhang (The Experimental Artist) noted a desire for a "save as template" feature so she wouldn't have to manually re-enter her complex "recipes" for experimental mixes.

User Evidence: Maya Singh (The Eco-Conscious Artist) identified a "blind spot" in the system: it provides no information on material sourcing, VOC levels, or sustainability, which forces her to do external research and decreases her trust in the system.

Architectural Impact: This limits the system’s Resilience in specialized markets where specific material data is required for the user's professional "Logic".

### Plan: Enhance Paint Order System Usability & Scalability

This plan addresses the identified issues: Inefficiency in Bulk Operations, Lack of Data Filtering and Reporting, and Missing Metadata and Customization Templates, primarily by modifying `streamlit_app_v3.py` and related database/menu logic.

**TL;DR:** We'll upgrade the database schema, add quantity input for bulk orders, implement filtering/search in View Orders, and prepare for richer menu item metadata by moving menu data to SQLite.

**Steps**

#### Phase 1: Database & Menu Data Enhancements (Foundational)

1.  **Update `orders` Database Schema for Quantity:**
    *   **Action:** Modify the `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS orders` statement in `init_db()` in `streamlit_app_v3.py` to include a `quantity INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 1` column.
    *   **Dependency:** This must be done before any new `INSERT` or `UPDATE` operations involving quantity.
    *   **Note:** Existing `orders.db` might need to be deleted/recreated for this schema change to take effect in development. For production, a schema migration would be needed, but for now, re-creation is fine.

2.  **Migrate paint_menu.txt to SQLite Database:**
    *   **Action:**
        *   Create a new SQLite table (e.g., `menu_items`) within `orders.db` (or a separate `menu.db` for separation) to store `paint_base`, `size`, `additives`, and `additive_parts` with additional metadata columns like `description`, `sustainability_info`, `origin`.
        *   Update PaintMenu.py: Modify `PaintMenu.from_file` to become `PaintMenu.from_db`, which connects to SQLite, initializes the `menu_items` table (if needed), populates it with default data if empty, and then loads data.
        *   Remove paint_menu.txt file and its auto-creation logic once migration is complete.
    *   **Relevant Files:** `streamlit_app_v3.py` (update `menu_file_path` usage, potentially `init_db` for new table), PaintMenu.py (major overhaul).
    *   **Benefit:** Enables storing rich metadata for menu items, solving "Missing Metadata."

#### Phase 2: Implement Bulk Operations & Order Duplication

1.  **Add Quantity Input in "Place Order":**
    *   **Action:** In `streamlit_app_v3.py`, within the `Place Order` form, add an `st.number_input` for `quantity` (defaulting to 1).
    *   **Modification:** Update the logic within the "Confirm and Save" button to iterate `quantity` times, calling `save_order()` for each item or modifying `save_order` to accept a quantity and insert multiple rows.
    *   **Relevant Files:** `streamlit_app_v3.py` (`Place Order` section, `save_order` function).
    *   **Benefit:** Addresses "Inefficiency in Bulk Operations."

2.  **Implement Order Duplication:**
    *   **Action:** In `streamlit_app_v3.py`, in the "View Orders" loop, add a "Duplicate" button next to each order.
    *   **Logic:** When "Duplicate" is clicked:
        *   Store the clicked order's details (paint base, size, additives, parts) into temporary `st.session_state` variables.
        *   Set `st.session_state.action = "Place Order"` and `st.rerun()`.
        *   In the "Place Order" form, check these session state variables and pre-fill the input fields if they exist.
    *   **Relevant Files:** `streamlit_app_v3.py` (`View Orders` section, `Place Order` section).
    *   **Benefit:** Addresses "Inefficiency in Bulk Operations" for repeating orders.

#### Phase 3: Data Filtering & Basic Reporting

1.  **Add Filtering/Search to "View Orders":**
    *   **Action:** In `streamlit_app_v3.py`, above the `st.dataframe` in the "View Orders" section, add:
        *   `st.text_input` for `search_artist` (search by first/last name).
        *   `st.selectbox` for `filter_paint_base` (dropdown of unique paint bases from loaded orders).
    *   **Modification:** Update `load_orders()` function to accept optional `search_artist` and `filter_paint_base` parameters. Adjust the `SELECT` SQL query in `load_orders()` to include `WHERE` clauses based on these parameters.
    *   **Relevant Files:** `streamlit_app_v3.py` (`View Orders` section, `load_orders` function).
    *   **Benefit:** Addresses "Lack of Data Filtering and Reporting."

2.  **Implement Basic Aggregated Reporting:**
    *   **Action:** In `streamlit_app_v3.py`, below the `st.dataframe` in "View Orders," add:

    *   **Relevant Files:** `streamlit_app_v3.py` (`View Orders` section, `load_orders` function or new reporting function).
